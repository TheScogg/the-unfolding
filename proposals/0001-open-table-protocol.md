# 0001: Open Table Protocol

- Status: **Draft - not adopted**
- Origin: the founding conversation initiated by TheScogg
- Scope: optional cooperation between communities
- Decision record: pending; no other community has agreed to this proposal

## Proposal

Develop a human-readable way for communities to explain what they offer, what participation means, and where their boundaries are. The software-interface analogy is an invitation to clarify capabilities and consent; it is not a claim that people or religions are software.

**Cooperation need not require agreement about ultimate truth.** Communities may share work and questions while remaining distinct.

## Three possible connections

1. **Shared action:** restore a stream, prepare meals, or care for grieving neighbors. Participants may give different reasons for the same work.
2. **Shared questions:** take turns hosting a question about generosity, doubt, a sacred life, or obligations to people outside one's community. Clear, respectful disagreement is a useful outcome.
3. **Shared ceremonies:** explain each ceremony's meaning and participation requirements in advance. Visitors may speak, observe, or decline. Do not imply that attendance expresses a belief or creates a commitment unless participants explicitly choose that meaning.

## A possible community description

```yaml
community: Example community
we_offer:
  - shared meals
  - neighborhood service
  - conversations about grief
visitors_may:
  - observe
  - ask questions
  - decline any activity
ask_us_before:
  - adapting our ceremonies
  - reproducing our sacred art
  - describing our beliefs publicly
participation_means:
  - hospitality, not conversion
disagreements:
  - recorded in each community's own words
```

This is an illustrative document, not an implemented technical protocol or a description of a real partner.

## Boundaries

Communities describe themselves. An adapter or agreement should be written with them. Partial cooperation is valuable: communities may work together on meals and remain unable to worship together. There is no expectation of progressively increasing access.

Within Church of the First Gift, the guiding proposal is:

> Our belief that you share in the gift gives us a responsibility toward you. It gives us no authority to explain you to yourself.

## Open questions

- How do communities approve, revise, or withdraw an agreement?
- How are minority views within each community heard?
- What practices cannot be translated or shared, and who decides?
- How can disagreements be recorded without exposing participants' private religious identities?
- How can people participate without a GitHub account or technical vocabulary?
