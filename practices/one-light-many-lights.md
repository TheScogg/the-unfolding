# One light, many lights

A shared ceremony for people who disagree about whether the giver survived the gift.

## Prepare

Explain the ceremony before beginning. Participation is voluntary. One light is placed at the center, with others around it. LED candles, a passed light, or another accessible representation can serve the same purpose. Use an arrangement appropriate to the setting and participants.

## Share the light

Light the others from the central candle, or represent that sharing with the chosen alternative. Let the room grow brighter as the first light is given away.

Then extinguish or turn off the original light.

The Last Light may say:

> The giver is gone.

The Living Whole may say:

> The giver has become the light among us.

The Questioning may remain silent, offer a question, or say:

> We share the light without settling the question.

Everyone uses their lights to illuminate the same table. Share a meal or a moment of attention if desired.

## Meaning and consent

The ceremony expresses this tradition's shared story and its unresolved question. It does not create a membership commitment. Visitors may observe or decline. Ask before inviting another community to adapt it or before describing their participation as an endorsement.
