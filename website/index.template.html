02 / The gift in daily life

Small acts.
An unfolding life.

We cannot repay creation.
We can participate in it.
These are invitations, never tests of worth.

01

Daily / The First Attention

Notice until it becomes remarkable.

Spend one quiet minute with a leaf, a familiar face, or the water in your glass. Ask: “What makes this possible?” No special feeling is required. Attention is enough.

02

Weekly / The Small Making

Add something good.

Create or mend something: a meal, a drawing, a repaired chair, a piece of code. Let it serve a life beyond your own. Rest and receiving care belong here, too.

03

Regularly / The Open Table

Share food. Share a question.

Bring a wonder, a difficulty, or an unanswered question. Participation and silence are equally welcome. Make room for someone new.

04

When needed / Making Room

Help another life flourish.

Offer attention, resources, or space. Listen carefully, restore a habitat, or step aside so someone else can lead. Give freely and respect your own limits.

05

Annually / The Honest Revision

Let yourself change.

Name a belief you changed, something you misunderstood, and one repair you still owe. This may remain private. Confession must never become a means of control.

A moment, right here

Take a minute.

Choose one ordinary thing nearby.
Give it your attention. Nothing else is required.

You can pause or stop whenever you wish.

03 / Room for disagreement

Did the giver
survive the gift?

The gift is our shared foundation.
The giver is our shared question.
No answer is a condition of belonging.

The Last Light

“God died so that
we could live.”

The sacrifice was complete. God no longer exists. We live among the consequences of a love whose source is gone. Prayer may be remembrance, gratitude, or a promise made among the living.

The hard question: can a gift retain meaning when its giver is gone?

The Living Whole

“God lives as everything
that became possible.”

God surrendered separate existence and remains present throughout creation. Every being participates in an ongoing divine life. Prayer may be communion with the sacred within and around us.

The hard question: if God remains in everything, what exactly was sacrificed?

?

The Questioning

“You may keep
the question open.”

Uncertainty is a legitimate position. People who cannot choose have equal standing and may lead gatherings. A person may change interpretations or remain uncertain throughout their life.

The shared question: what can we make possible before we agree?

Our covenant

No answer grants authority over another person.
No doubt removes a person from the table.
Every teaching may be questioned.
Every person may leave.

A shared ceremony

One light.
Many meanings.

One candle lights the others. Then the original light is extinguished.

One person says, “The giver is gone.” Another says, “The giver has become the light among us.” Others keep the question open.

Everyone uses their lights to illuminate the same table.
