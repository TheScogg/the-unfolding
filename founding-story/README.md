# The First Gift

Before there were stars, there was God.

Within God lay every possible thing: every ocean, every creature, every stranger who might someday become a friend. But possibility had no life of its own.

God desired that something beyond himself should truly exist.

To make room for it, God surrendered the wholeness of his own being. What had been one became many. This was the Great Sacrifice: the beginning we call the Big Bang.

The gift unfolded into space and matter. Stars gathered, burned, and scattered their substance. Worlds formed. On at least one world, matter became living things. Living things learned to feel, to wonder, to make, and to recognize suffering in one another.

At last, the universe could ask where it came from.

Some answered: "God died so that we could live."

Others answered: "God lives as everything that became possible."

And others kept the question open.

All inherited the same world. All faced the same task: deciding what to do with the gift.

*The Unfolding continues wherever we make room for another life to flourish.*

## Reading the story

This is the founding spiritual narrative of a proposed tradition. It gives meaning to cosmic origins; it does not claim scientific proof of divine intention or replace scientific inquiry. Its details may be read as theology, sacred poetry, or an invitation to wonder.

The open question at its ending is intentional. See the [interpretations](../interpretations/README.md). The common response is a practice of [wonder, making, and care](../practices/README.md).

> God did not stand apart. God gave himself away.
